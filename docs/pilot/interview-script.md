# Pilot Interview Script — Occountant (C11)

Structured interviews at **day 7, day 14, and day 30**. Goal: learn whether Occountant solves a real
problem, whether they trust it, whether they'll keep using it, and whether they'll pay — **without
leading them**. Ask open questions; shut up and listen; record verbatim quotes.

**Skeptical-operator rules:**
- Do **not** pitch or defend the product. You're collecting evidence, not selling.
- Ask about **what they actually did**, not what they think of features.
- A polite "it's nice" is **not** evidence. Retention + willingness-to-pay + a concrete reason is.
- Write down the exact words. Note where they hesitated or contradicted earlier praise.

---

## Day 7 — "Did you get value at all?"

1. Walk me through what you did with Occountant this week. *(Let them narrate; note tasks completed.)*
2. What was the **first thing** you tried? Did it work the way you expected?
3. Where did you get **stuck or confused**? *(Probe: which screen? what did you expect to happen?)*
4. Did you issue a real invoice? Send it to a customer? What did they say, if anything?
5. Did anything look **wrong** in a number? *(Highest priority — chase any yes.)*
6. Did you make a backup? Do you feel your data is safe? Why / why not?
7. What did you use **instead of** Occountant this week (spreadsheet, notebook, other app, accountant)?
8. On a scale of "delete it today" to "would miss it" — where are you, and why?

*Decision to record:* keep going / needs help / at risk of dropping.

## Day 14 — "Is it becoming a habit?"

1. How many invoices / payments / expenses did you enter since we last spoke? *(Numbers.)*
2. Are you using it for **all** your invoicing now, or only some? What's still done elsewhere, and why?
3. What's the **one thing** that most slows you down or annoys you?
4. Is there anything you needed to do that Occountant **couldn't**? *(Log as a request → product-decisions.)*
5. Have you had to fix a mistake (cancel/void an invoice, correct a payment)? Could you figure it out?
6. Did you produce anything for your **accountant or tax** (report/CSV/PDF)? Did it work for them?
7. Anything make you **nervous** about your data or the numbers?

*Decision to record:* retained / partial / churning. Count actual transactions entered.

## Day 30 — "Will you keep it, and pay?"

1. Are you still using it? For everything, or partially? *(Confirm against their transaction count.)*
2. What has Occountant **replaced** for you? What problem does it actually solve for your business?
3. If Occountant disappeared tomorrow, what would you do? How much would that hurt (1–10)?
4. **Pricing (don't anchor):** "If you were to pay for this, what would feel fair for a business your
   size?" *(Let them say a number first.)* Then: one-time purchase, or monthly, or yearly — which fits
   how you think about tools like this?
5. What would have to be **true** for you to pay for it? What's missing or unproven for you?
6. Would you **recommend** it to another business like yours? Which kind of business, specifically?
7. What's the single most important thing we should change before charging money?

*Decisions to record:* retention (yes/partial/no), willing-to-pay (yes/maybe/no + number + reason),
the **one** blocker to conversion, and which business type they'd refer.

## After each interview — fill the discovery log
Transfer to `docs/pilot-discovery-log.md`: tasks completed unaided, confusing screens, missing
workflows, requested features, trust quotes, retention, willingness-to-pay + reason. Move any feature
request into `docs/product-decisions.md` with a class and a distinct-business count.

## What counts as real evidence (be strict)
- **Retention:** they entered real transactions in weeks 3–4 without prompting.
- **Value:** they can name the concrete thing it replaced and what it would cost them to lose it.
- **Willingness to pay:** a number **and** a reason **and** ideally a signed intent — not "sure,
  maybe." One genuine yes with a clear reason beats five polite maybes.
